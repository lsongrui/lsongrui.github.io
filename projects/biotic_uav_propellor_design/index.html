Abstract#

I proposed and led a seven-person project on a bionic propeller inspired by humpback-whale fins. My work covered 3D modeling and CFD simulation.

We compared three airfoil candidates, designed and checked the propeller, produced a printed prototype, and tested it on a ground cart. The measured cart acceleration provided a limited experimental comparison with the conventional propeller. The project received Second Prize in the China College Students’ “Internet+” Innovation Competition, as recorded in my CV.

Key pictures and conclusions#

Airfoil simulation#

CFD simulation of three kinds of straight airfoil are performed:

  • The CFD comparisons examined drag and lift-to-drag ratio across the airfoil candidates.
  • The sinusoidal leading-edge candidate was selected for the propeller model.

3D Propeller design#

modeling#

  • Leading edge: $-45 z=0.5/12\times9.3133\times sin(0.3373\times3\times y)$

  • Torsion center: wing center

  • Torsion angle: $-45$-$0$ degrees linear distribution

The selected airfoil formed the basis of the three-dimensional propeller.

The reported strength calculation gave a critical rotational speed of $n_{max} = 7339.27 r/min$
